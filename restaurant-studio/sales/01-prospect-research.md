# 01. Prospect research

Goal: find independent restaurants where you can see a real, specific problem on a phone, and record exactly what you saw.

## Who to look for

- Independent, locally owned. Not chains or franchises (they have corporate sites).
- First priority: sushi, hibachi, Japanese, Korean BBQ. Then any small independent restaurant.
- In Topeka or Ithaca, or near enough to visit.

Where to look: Google Maps ("sushi near me", "korean bbq"), the local paper's food section, walking a main street, delivery apps (only to find names, never to contact through them).

## What counts as a real problem

Something a hungry guest on a phone would run into:

- The menu is a PDF or photo you have to pinch and zoom to read.
- No menu online at all, or prices clearly out of date.
- Hours on the site disagree with Google.
- The order or reservation button is missing or does not work.
- The phone number is not tappable.
- No website, only a Facebook page or a delivery-app listing.
- Catering is mentioned, but the only way to ask is to call during service.

Not a real problem: "the design looks old", "I would use different colors". Taste is not a reason to contact anyone.

## The phone check (5 minutes, required before any contact)

Use your own phone, on cellular, not wifi if you can.

1. Search the restaurant's name on Google and on Google Maps. Note the website link, if any. **If research says "no website found", look yourself.** Search has missed real sites before.
2. Open the site. Does it load in a few seconds?
3. **Menu:** can you read it without zooming? Is it text or an image or PDF?
4. **Hours:** do they match Google Maps? Is there a holiday note?
5. **Order or reserve:** tap the link. Does it open the right system for the right location?
6. **Phone:** tap the number. Does your phone offer to dial it? (Hang up before it rings.)
7. **Address:** does it open in maps?
8. Take one or two screenshots for your notes.

## The site-audit tool (not built yet)

A small laptop tool that captures a phone-size screenshot and a few factual checks was planned for `tools/site-audit/`, but it has not been built. Until it exists, the phone check above is the whole audit. Even once it is built, it will only help you remember what you saw. It does not replace looking at the site on your own phone.

## Record it

In the CRM Prospects tab ([../tools/crm/README.md](../tools/crm/README.md)):

- **Checked on my phone:** today's date.
- **What I confirmed:** plain facts. "Menu is a 3-page PDF, text too small to read without zooming." Not "menu is bad."
- **Observed issue:** the one thing you would lead with.
- **Fit (1 to 5):** how clearly we could help.
- **Outreach status:** "Checked and ready", or leave it "Not checked yet".

If the site is fine, write that down and move on. That saves you from contacting them later.

## Do not

- Do not collect personal emails, cell numbers or social profiles of staff or owners.
- Do not log in to anything, fill in their forms as a test, or place a fake order.
- Do not assume a site is bad from search results or someone else's notes.
- Do not research more than you can contact this week. Notes go stale.
