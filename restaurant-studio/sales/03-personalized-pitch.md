# 03. The personalized pitch

Turn one thing you saw into a short "here is what I would change" note. Use it as the middle of an email, as what you say in person, or as the "What I saw" part of a proposal.

## The formula

1. **What I saw**, in plain facts, on my own phone, with the date.
2. **Why it matters to a guest**, in one sentence.
3. **What I would change**, small and specific.
4. **What it would cost**, if they ask.

Rules: only things you saw yourself. No judgment words ("outdated", "broken", "bad"). Never say it will bring more customers. If you are not sure, ask instead of telling.

## Example 1: menu is a PDF or a photo

> On {{date}} I opened your menu on my phone. It's a PDF, and I had to zoom in to read the prices.
> Guests often check the menu in the car or in line, one-handed.
> I would put the full menu on the page as text, by section, with prices, so it reads at normal size and you can update a price without redoing a file.
> That's part of the Starter site ($699).

## Example 2: no website, only a Facebook page

> When I searched for {{restaurant}} on Google and Maps on my phone, I found your Facebook page but no website. (Check this twice. Search has missed real sites before.)
> Some guests do not use Facebook, and posts push the menu and hours out of sight.
> I would build a one-page site with your menu, hours, address, a tap-to-call button and directions, on a domain in your name. You can keep posting on Facebook as you do now.
> Starter, $699.

## Example 3: hours or links out of date

> On {{date}} your website showed {{hours_on_site}}, and Google Maps showed {{hours_on_maps}}. I wasn't sure which was right. The Order button also {{what_happened}}.
> A guest who sees the wrong hours may not come, or may come when you're closed.
> I would fix the hours in one place with an "Open now" indicator and holiday hours, and make the order button open the system you already use.
> If you already have a site you're happy with, sometimes this is just an hour of fixes. If you want a fresh build, Starter is $699, and maintenance at $59 a month covers changes like these.

## Example 4: catering offered, but only by phone

> Your site says you cater, and the only way I could find to ask was to call.
> Calls about catering often come in during service, when nobody can take down the details.
> I would add a catering form that saves each request to a Google Sheet you own, emails you right away, and tells the customer it arrived. It never confirms a date, availability or price. That stays with you.
> The Catering Inquiry Automation add-on is $349, with either website package.

## Example 5: the site is fine

> I looked at your site on my phone. The menu is easy to read, the hours match Google, and ordering works.
> Honestly, you don't need a new website from me.
> If you ever want help keeping it updated, here's my card.

Then mark them in the CRM with a note, and move on. Being honest here is part of the pitch for the next restaurant.
