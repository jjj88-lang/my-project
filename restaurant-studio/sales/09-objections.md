# 09. Honest answers to real objections

Listen to the whole concern first. Answer briefly and truthfully. If the honest answer is "you don't need this", say it.

**"It costs too much."**
> That's fair. It's a real expense. Starter is $699 once, paid half now and half at approval, and you own everything after. There's no monthly fee unless you want me to host and update it. If it doesn't fit this year, that's okay. I'd rather you wait than stretch.

**"I already have a website."**
> Great. Is it easy to update, and does the menu read well on a phone? *(If yes:)* Then you probably don't need me. *(If not sure:)* Want me to show you what I saw on my phone? Sometimes it's a small fix, not a new site.

**"My nephew (or a friend) does it."**
> That's a good setup if it works for you. The only thing I'd check is that the domain is registered in your name, so you're never stuck if they get busy. Happy to show you how to check that, free.

**"We're on Facebook and DoorDash. That's enough."**
> For a lot of places it mostly is. A website adds one page you control: your menu at your prices, your hours, your phone number, and a link to order however you prefer. Delivery apps and Facebook can change how they show your page; your own site doesn't. If your regulars find you fine now, it may not be worth it yet.

**"I have no time."**
> I understand. Running a restaurant doesn't leave much. From you I need about an hour total: send a menu, hours and a logo, then look at a draft twice. I do the rest. If even that's too much right now, I can check back after the season.

**"Will it bring me more customers?"**
> I can't promise that, and I'd be suspicious of anyone who does. What it does: guests who look you up see the right menu, hours and phone number, can tap to call or get directions, and can reach your ordering link easily. It looks professional, and when something changes, it's easy to update.

**"I've been burned by a web person before."**
> I'm sorry. That's common, and it's usually about control. With me, the domain is registered in your name and your account. Your content is yours. If you stop maintenance, I hand over the site files with 30 days' notice. You pay half up front and half only when you've approved the site. It's all in a short written agreement.

**"Can you do it cheaper?"**
> The prices are the same for everyone. What I can do is scope it down: Starter is one page, and you can skip maintenance and take the files instead. If even that's too much, a free option is making sure your Google Business Profile has your current hours and menu link.

**"Let me think about it."**
> Of course. Is there something specific you want to think through? I'm happy to answer it now or by email. Otherwise I'll check back once next week, and if it's not the right time, no problem.
Then follow [04-follow-up.md](04-follow-up.md), and stop after the second follow-up.

## When the right answer is "you probably don't need this"

- Their site already reads well on a phone, hours are right, links work, and someone updates it.
- They're closing, selling, or about to change concepts.
- They mostly serve regulars and are happy with it.
- They can't comfortably afford it this year.

Say it plainly, leave your card, and note it in the CRM. That honesty is what makes people refer you later.
